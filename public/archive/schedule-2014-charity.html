How do I begin saying thank you to 300 men and their families (many of whom I don’t even know!) who have given so generously, supported us with open arms and showed us compassion and empathy with such a memorable tribute to Pat. I will forever hold the vision of my girls and I at the centre diamond, my parents and inlaws behind the fence, Johnny giving an amazing speech, Mike taking photos, the Rangers on the home base line, and 300 men gathered around at shallow infield, wearing a “rainbow” of baseball jerseys, clapping for us, crying, and remembering Pat. The sky was blue, the sunshine bright and the breeze warm. The sights, the sounds, the feelings and the words will be warmly remembered and treasured by our family. Each of my daughters given the opportunity to throw a “first pitch” at the annual charity ball tournament, in our honour, and the beyond generous donation to the girls’ “scholarship” fund. Pat and I used to joke that with 3 little girls their future educations would be left to “innie-meanie-minny-moe” or “Rock, Paper, Scissors”!! Please know that Pat would be honoured to know that you all cared so much for him and for our girls that you did this for us. And also know, that if the tables were turned, Pat would do in kind what you have all done for him. Generosity, goodwill and charity were a big part of his character and will forever be his legacy.

Unfortunately the concept of “pay it forward” has been returned to Pat. For those of you who don’t know, Pat and are also raising his younger brother, Laclin, who is now 19. When Pat’s Mom was sick with cancer, he would go back to Winnipeg for a week every month for 8 months, to help take care of his Mom and mentor Laclin, who was then 11 years old. We made connections with his school, social workers and community in order to support Laclin while their mom was battling her disease. When their Mom passed away, Pat and I adopted Laclin and he became a big brother to Falynn who was a toddler then, and then Waverlea joined our family a few months later. As the years go by, people always tell Pat and I that we are good people for taking Laclin in and raising him. But for us, it was never that we sacrificed or did the right thing with Laclin, it was what we needed to do and we never looked back or regretted the choices we made.

In a cruel twist of fate, Laclin and I are going through this again. Never in a million years, would I have ever expected to be a widow at 35, raising 4 kids. But I know that I’m not alone. I learned a while ago to put my own ego aside and accept the help that people offer. Pat and I have always done it, and I know that I will do it again, but right now it’s our turn. So thank you for being part of my “village”. There is the old adage that it takes a village to raise a child, but in our case, I think it takes a city/nation/country!!

I’ve mentioned it to a couple of the guys, but I will say it again. When Pat and I moved to Markham he “shopped’ around for a ball league to join. He’d been a part of many teams and leagues over the years and since we knew that Markham would be our forever home, he wanted to be a part of a good, solid group, who was fun, and focussed on fellowship, rather than politics of a fun sport. Pat joined 3 Markham leagues that summer (and with 3 kids I threatened he choose 1 for the next summer – it was me or baseball!!). In the one league, he was part of a winning team, they were season champs. At their annual banquet he received the trophy, the plaque and the coveted jacket. Despite the winning team and great sponsorship, Pat chose the MMSPL for the great bunch of guys, great baseball, post game beers, socials and tournaments. He never looked back. The league has been a huge part in helping us create a home in Markham; by fostering friendships, playdates and community connections. A home is so much more than a place where you live, it is where relationships are built and memories created.

To all the guys in the league, thank you. Thank you for what you’ve done, and will continue to do to in Patrick’s honour. I will do my best to help foster Paddy’s love of baseball with our girls. I’ve written all the Rangers early games in my calendar and we’ll get to as many of those games as we can, even if it means I’ve got one foot at the park and the other foot in the stands. I know you guys will continue to invite us to your post game socials, tournaments and banquet. The girls have 300 new “uncles” who will be there to tickle them, watch them swim, push them on swings, sit on their laps, play catch, and walk their dogs! I hope that I can ask you guys be my 300 “honeys” – I’ve got a 2 year honey-do list that’s been neglected since Pat got sick (as long as your wives and partners can spare you from your own honey-do lists!!) To the guys who I don’t know as well, thank you for your hugs, your condolences, for wearing your jerseys with pride at the visitation and funeral service. I ask that when you see me or the girls around town (at the grocery store, mall, restaurant, for a walk, or most likely the beer store!), that you take a moment to say hi (even if i don’t recognise you) and introduce yourself to us again so that my girls can see the “team” that is Daddy’s legacy.
